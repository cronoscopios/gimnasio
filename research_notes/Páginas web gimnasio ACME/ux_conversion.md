# UX and conversion best practices for gym websites (for Gimnasio ACME)

Note on evidence quality: almost all available evidence is **vendor or agency marketing** (gym software companies, web design agencies). No peer-reviewed or independent A/B study of gym-website conversion was found. General web-performance data comes from Google (web.dev), which is independent of the fitness industry. Consumer gym-choice data comes from YouGov and Les Mills (Les Mills is an industry vendor).

## 1. Which pages and sections most influence conversion for gyms?

### Takeaway
Sources agree on a core set that drives sign-ups: a trial or first-visit offer with a clear CTA, visible pricing (or at least a "desde X €/mes" price), an embedded class schedule you can book from, real photos of the facility and members, reviews and testimonials, and location and hours. Cost and location are what consumers weigh most when choosing a gym, so those must be easy to find.

### Cited Findings
- Consumer choice drivers (US): cost 57%, location 53%, cleanliness 49%. Among current gym-goers, 61% cited cost. — [YouGov](https://business.yougov.com/content/41118-us-people-are-returning-gym-what-do-consumers-valu)
- Older UK data (2010, dated): more than two-thirds named proximity to home as the main factor, and 64% named a reasonable price. — [Health Club Management](https://www.healthclubmanagement.co.uk/health-club-management-news/Report-Gym-location-most-pressing-issue/236673)
- Les Mills (vendor) says more than 12,000 gym prospects name people (staff and instructors) as a key reason to join. Its 2026 Global Fitness Report (10,000 consumers) found 51% of members bring friends once they find a workout they like. — [Les Mills Global Fitness Report](https://lesmills.com/uk/global-fitness-report)
- 88% of consumers research a local business online before visiting (BrightLocal, cited by Gymdesk). — [Gymdesk](https://gymdesk.com/blog/9-gym-website-ideas-what-makes-a-good-gym-website)
- Gymdesk (vendor) lists the elements that convert: a trial CTA above the fold, real photos rather than stock, pricing visible without contacting the gym (or a "starting at" price), an embedded schedule with 1-click or 2-tap booking and no forced account creation, and the Google review count and rating plus named testimonials above the fold. — [Gymdesk](https://gymdesk.com/blog/9-gym-website-ideas-what-makes-a-good-gym-website)
- Gymdesk benchmark: if fewer than 5% of schedule-page visitors confirm a booking, there is friction in the flow. No source is given for this figure. — [Gymdesk](https://gymdesk.com/blog/9-gym-website-ideas-what-makes-a-good-gym-website)
- StudioMeyer (agency, Spanish-language) recommends a persistent "reservar prueba" CTA, visible location and hours, a trial-class funnel of no more than 3 clicks, and all membership options listed with prices. — [StudioMeyer](https://studiomeyer.io/es/blog/webdesign-fitness-studios)
- A 2025 academic paper (PICBE) hypothesizes that transparent content and pricing plus strong mobile performance improve UX and conversion. The excerpt seen states hypotheses, not confirmed results. — [PICBE 2025](https://reference-global.com/2/v2/download/article/10.2478/picbe-2025-0311.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a single-location gym in Spain, the essential pages are: Inicio, Tarifas/Precios, Horario de clases (bookable), Actividades/Clases, Instalaciones (real photos, optional virtual tour), Equipo/Entrenadores, Prueba gratis/Pase de día (landing page), Contacto/Ubicación (map, hours, parking, phone/WhatsApp), and Área de socios/App (a link to the management software portal). Legal pages (aviso legal, privacidad, cookies) are required in Spain anyway.
- The trainers page supports the Les Mills "people drive joining" finding. It is worth having, but it is secondary to pricing, schedule and trial.

### Gaps
- No independent study ranks individual gym pages by conversion contribution.
- No evidence was found on how much virtual tours or 360° photos affect gym conversion specifically.
- No Spanish consumer survey on gym choice factors from 2022-2026 was found (an HFA or Spanish source such as the Life Fitness / Valgo market reports could be checked).

## 2. Show prices publicly or use lead forms?

### Takeaway
Most fitness sources recommend showing prices, or at least a starting price. The main counter-argument (hide prices to force a contact) comes from agencies serving martial arts, dance and youth studios, rests only on anecdotes, and admits it increases unqualified leads. For a mid-market single-location gym where cost is the top choice factor, publishing prices is the better-supported choice.

### Cited Findings
- Codivox 2026 audit of 36 US gym websites (cited by an agency page): pricing transparency was the weakest category, averaging 46.4/100, and 94.4% of sites did not state contract terms. The same page cites gym landing-page conversion of 26.0% in the top decile against an 11.2% average. These are agency marketing figures with unverified methodology. — [Webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/sub-services/gym-web-design)
- Gymdesk counts "pricing hide-and-seek" ("contact us for pricing") as a top mistake because it prevents visitors from qualifying themselves. It notes that big-box premium brands (Equinox, Life Time) hide pricing entirely. — [Gymdesk](https://gymdesk.com/blog/9-gym-website-ideas-what-makes-a-good-gym-website)
- StoreRocket says prospects who can't find prices tend to leave for a competitor that shows them, and recommends at least starting prices and tiers. — [StoreRocket](https://storerocket.io/learn/gym-marketing)
- Nanoglobals says pricing should be reachable within 2 clicks. Value gyms should show prices, while high-end gyms may gate them behind a visit or consultation. — [Nanoglobals](https://nanoglobals.com/gym-websites/)
- Ueni says publishing prices converts better than hiding them behind a form, but gives no data. — [Ueni](https://ueni.com/blog/gym-fitness-websites/)
- KOM (Peruvian agency, Spanish-language) argues that visitors who already know the price arrive ready to sign up rather than to ask questions, and recommends showing monthly, quarterly and annual plans. — [KOM](https://kom.pe/landing-page-gimnasio-peru/)
- Against: Market Muscles (vendor) claims that removing pricing and schedules produced "a dramatic increase in leads", because a price without context deters people and hidden information forces contact. The article has no statistics and explicitly targets martial arts schools, dance studios and youth programs. — [Market Muscles](https://marketmuscles.com/2026/05/why-keeping-your-pricing-and-schedule-off-your-website-generates-more-leads/)
- Nuanced view: Two-Brain Business says hiding prices brings in more prospects who don't know your value. That helps a new or high-turnover gym with strong sales skills. Established gyms, or owners who can't handle price objections, should show prices, and gating can make clients distrust the gym. No data. — [Two-Brain Business](https://twobrainbusiness.com/prices-gym-website/)
- A podcast cites "HubSpot research" claiming a 23% revenue increase for studios with clear pricing. Unverified, so do not rely on it. — [Fit to Grit Cast](https://fittogritcast.buzzsprout.com/336032/episodes/17388722-why-fitness-studio-growth-depends-on-experience)

### Inferences
- Recommendation for ACME: publish the main tarifas (monthly price, matrícula, permanencia, what's included, and any contract terms, since these are rarely stated), and pair them with a clear "Prueba gratis" CTA so price-sensitive visitors still enter the funnel. Personal training or premium services can use "desde X €" plus a form.
- Low-cost chains that compete in Spain typically show prices prominently and offer online sign-up. This is general market knowledge and was not verified in this research.

### Gaps
- No controlled test (A/B) of shown vs hidden gym pricing was found.

## 3. Home page best practices (hero, CTA, social proof, location/hours)

### Takeaway
The hero should show the real facility and members, with a short, action-verb trial CTA visible without scrolling on mobile. Review rating and count should sit near the top, and location, hours and contact should be easy to reach.

### Cited Findings
- Gymdesk: the trial CTA should be visible within roughly the top 600-700px on a phone, use 5 words or fewer, and start with an action verb. Hero images should show the real space and members, not stock photos. The Google review rating and count should appear above the fold. — [Gymdesk](https://gymdesk.com/blog/9-gym-website-ideas-what-makes-a-good-gym-website)
- Gymdesk names "the brochure" (no above-fold CTA and no way to book) and "the stock-photo mirage" as common failures. — [Gymdesk](https://gymdesk.com/blog/9-gym-website-ideas-what-makes-a-good-gym-website)
- StudioMeyer: a persistent CTA plus visible location and hours. — [StudioMeyer](https://studiomeyer.io/es/blog/webdesign-fitness-studios)

### Inferences
- Suggested home page structure for ACME: hero (real photo or video, "Prueba gratis" CTA, secondary "Ver tarifas"), Google rating strip, a price preview ("desde X €/mes"), featured activities, the schedule preview, facility gallery, trainers, testimonials, then a map with hours and contact (WhatsApp is common in Spain), and a final CTA.

### Gaps
- No independent studies on hero design specific to gyms were found. The guidance is practitioner consensus.

## 4. Campaign landing pages (promociones, matrícula gratis, pase de prueba)

### Takeaway
Dedicated trial or offer landing pages are widely recommended and reportedly lift lead capture. Conversion to membership depends mostly on structured follow-up. Published trial-to-member figures range from about 20% to 50%, and all of them come from vendors.

### Cited Findings
- KOM (agency) claims landing pages with a free-trial hook reach 8-15% visitor-to-lead conversion, against 2-3% without one. The figures are not independently sourced. — [KOM](https://kom.pe/landing-page-gimnasios/)
- Rework (vendor resource) says structured trial programs convert about 30-45%, against 10-20% for gyms without a formal protocol. It cites Mindbody data that 7-day trials convert better than 3-day trials (35% vs 27%). — [Rework](https://resources.rework.com/es/libraries/gym-fitness-growth/free-trial-day-pass-strategies)
- Glofox (vendor) says trial-to-member conversion ranges from 20% to 50% (sourced to a Reddit thread) and that published benchmarks are scarce. Paid trials ($10-30 per week) reportedly convert slightly better. Follow-up should start "within hours of the trial, not days." — [Glofox](https://www.glofox.com/blog/gym-trial-conversion-rates/)
- Glofox also has a separate article arguing "the free gym trial is dead", favouring paid or structured intro offers. — [Glofox](https://www.glofox.com/blog/why-the-free-gym-trial-is-dead/)
- Zenoti notes that many trial users leave when the trial expires without consistent follow-up. — [Zenoti](https://www.zenoti.com/es/thecheckin/from-free-trials-to-full-membership-using-software-to-convert-more-gym-leads)
- Webtonic cites top-decile gym landing pages converting at 26.0% against an 11.2% average. Agency source. — [Webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/sub-services/gym-web-design)

### Inferences
- ACME should have reusable landing-page templates: "Prueba gratis / pase de día", "Matrícula gratis (promo del mes)" and seasonal campaigns (septiembre, enero). Each needs one goal, a short form (name, phone/email, preferred day), RGPD consent, and no main navigation. The site should also capture campaign source (UTM) and trigger quick follow-up (email or WhatsApp).

### Gaps
- No Spain-specific data on trial or matrícula-gratis conversion was found.

## 5. Online booking, member area, and gym management software in Spain

### Takeaway
Users expect to book classes on the site or in an app without friction: no PDF schedules, no forced account creation just to view the schedule. Mindbody and Virtuagym document embeddable widgets. For the software most used in Spain (Trainingym, Resawod, Aimharder, Provis), no public documentation of embeddable widgets was found. These platforms mainly provide their own member app and portal, so the website will likely link to them or use whatever embed they supply on request.

### Cited Findings
- Gymdesk: the schedule should be embedded on the site with 1-click or 2-tap booking, with no PDF, external login or forced account creation. It claims embedded booking "consistently outperforms" linked booking but gives no number. — [Gymdesk](https://gymdesk.com/blog/9-gym-website-ideas-what-makes-a-good-gym-website)
- Mindbody "Branded Web Tools": embeddable widgets for the class schedule, appointments, staff profiles, account sign-up and contact forms. They work with WordPress, Wix and Squarespace, colours are customisable, they support multiple languages and currencies, and payment happens on the page. — [Mindbody](https://www.mindbodyonline.com/branded-web-tools)
- A third-party agency claims Mindbody widgets impose their own fonts and layout and aren't always optimised for mobile. Self-interested source. — [IP Studio](https://ipstudio.co/mindbody-websites)
- Virtuagym advertises a "plug-and-play online booking widget" for websites plus lead generation features. — [Virtuagym](https://business.virtuagym.com/blog/updates-and-improvements-scheduling/); [Virtuagym scheduling](https://business.virtuagym.com/solutions/scheduling-software/)
- Gymdesk documents a schedule embed (a script plus an HTML element), a booking widget and standalone sign-up links. — [Gymdesk docs](https://docs.gymdesk.com/help/website-integration)
- Trainingym is described as the most widely used software in Spain (a competitor blog makes this claim). It offers members, bookings, access control, billing and a custom member app, and its pricing is not public. — [PTminder blog](https://blog.ptminder.com/mejor-software-gestion-gimnasios-espana); [Fitnova](https://www.fitnova.eu/blog/mejor-software-gimnasios)
- Trainingym's own comparison lists "Página web" and "App personalizada" among its solutions and gives no price. The comparison is self-published. — [Trainingym blog](https://blog.trainingym.com/es/blog/mejores-softwares-gimnasios)
- Resawod targets functional training and CrossFit boxes. It has class booking, vouchers (bonos), access control and separate coach and member apps, from €72/month for up to 100 active athletes. — [Resawod prices](https://resawod.com/en/prices/); [EFAD](https://www.efadeporte.com/blog/gestion-deportiva/softwares-de-gestion-de-centros-deportivos)
- AimHarder's GetApp listing says schedules integrate with your website, without details. — [GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/recreation-wellness-software/a/aimharder/)

### Inferences
- For a Laravel-built site, the practical options are: (a) embed the vendor's widget or iframe if one exists, (b) link "Reservar clase" and "Área de socios" to the vendor's portal or app (common with Trainingym, Resawod and Aimharder), or (c) build a lightweight custom schedule page fed by the vendor API, if one exists. ACME should confirm with its chosen vendor before designing the booking UX.
- Aimharder and Resawod suit boxes and functional training. Trainingym, Provis and Virtuagym suit a traditional multi-activity gym.

### Gaps
- No public documentation was found for embeddable widgets or APIs from Trainingym, Aimharder, Resawod, Provis or Glofox. Ask vendors directly.
- No data was found on Spanish users' 2025-2026 expectations for member areas specifically.

## 6. Mobile-first behaviour and page speed

### Takeaway
Most gym-site traffic is mobile, and slow pages lose roughly half of mobile visitors. The target is under about 2.5-3 seconds on mobile with passing Core Web Vitals, with special attention to the schedule page.

### Cited Findings
- More than 76% of website traffic is mobile (Statista, cited by Gymdesk). — [Gymdesk](https://gymdesk.com/blog/9-gym-website-ideas-what-makes-a-good-gym-website)
- 53% of mobile visitors leave if a page loads slowly (Google, web.dev). Pages loading in 1 second convert about 3 times better than pages loading in 5 seconds. — [Gymdesk citing web.dev](https://gymdesk.com/blog/9-gym-website-ideas-what-makes-a-good-gym-website)
- Core Web Vitals have been a ranking signal since 2021, and INP replaced FID in 2024 (Google Search Central). — [Gymdesk](https://gymdesk.com/blog/9-gym-website-ideas-what-makes-a-good-gym-website)
- Mobile specs: single-column layout, tap targets of at least 44px, no hover-dependent states, CTAs in the thumb zone. — [Gymdesk](https://gymdesk.com/blog/9-gym-website-ideas-what-makes-a-good-gym-website)
- StudioMeyer: load in under 2.5 seconds, especially the schedule page. — [StudioMeyer](https://studiomeyer.io/es/blog/webdesign-fitness-studios)

### Inferences
- Use a sticky mobile CTA bar (Prueba gratis / WhatsApp / Llamar), optimised hero images (WebP/AVIF), and avoid heavy third-party iframes above the fold.

### Gaps
- No fitness-specific Spanish mobile traffic statistic was found.

## 7. Common mistakes in gym websites

### Takeaway
The recurring failures are: no clear CTA or booking path, hidden pricing, PDF or outdated schedules, stock photos, slow mobile pages, and contract terms that aren't stated.

### Cited Findings
- Gymdesk's four mistakes: "The brochure" (no CTA or booking), "stock-photo mirage", "pricing hide-and-seek", and "schedule dead-end" (PDFs, external logins, outdated images). — [Gymdesk](https://gymdesk.com/blog/9-gym-website-ideas-what-makes-a-good-gym-website)
- 94.4% of 36 audited US gym sites did not state contract terms (Codivox via agency). — [Webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/sub-services/gym-web-design)
- KOM recommends keeping the class schedule current and showing full plan numbers. — [KOM](https://kom.pe/pagina-web-para-gimnasios-web-lima/)
- Slow mobile loading leads to abandonment (53%). — [Gymdesk citing web.dev](https://gymdesk.com/blog/9-gym-website-ideas-what-makes-a-good-gym-website)

### Inferences
- Other likely mistakes, not sourced here: no RGPD-compliant forms or cookie banner, no WhatsApp or phone CTA (important in Spain), and a missing or inconsistent Google Business Profile link.

### Gaps
- No Spain-specific audit of gym websites was found.
